# Mr. Feast's Mansion

## Pitch

A first-person parody-horror game in which a contestant enters a million-dollar reality challenge inside a lavish mansion, discovers that losing contestants are being served to a secret elite audience, and turns the show's own house and production systems against its host.

## Core gameplay loop

Alternate between sanctioned reality-show competitions and separate free-roam investigation periods. During investigation, inspect suspicious objects, connect clues left by previous contestants, recover tools and keys, unlock restricted evidence, sabotage production systems, and hide from or evade Mr. Feast as the show becomes openly hostile. The current clue spine begins with one subtly misfiled book among the Library shelves, sends the player to the garden for a shovel and the hedge maze for a buried basement key, then continues through the locked basement to the Archive and keypad-gated Workroom. Once the player knows the XIII clue and carries the shovel, the maze becomes mostly dark and repeatedly stirs around the player's current corridor. Three cells inside, living hedge seals close both entrances and persist through a save restored in the maze; early exploration and sanctioned Storm Run traversal remain safe and open. The carried flashlight stays reliable. Recovering B-13 immediately releases both portals and turns the sustained haunt into one longer chamber scare without revealing its source.

## Game rules

- A fresh run opens with Mr. Feast waiting just inside the front door to deliver a slow, suspicious reality-show briefing: the player is competing for one million dollars, selected public rooms and grounds are introduced, cameras are normalized, and the basement is explicitly forbidden. Player movement and ordinary interactions remain locked until the complete welcome ends, but an immediately available `Skip intro` control dismisses the complete speech sequence and releases exploration without changing story state. Mr. Feast then resumes his foyer patrol and exploration begins without exposing the hidden Contestant 13 objective.
- The first sanctioned competition is `Feast Says`. Mr. Feast calls it after ten active minutes of post-welcome exploration or immediately after the player's first major clue discovery. The triggering clue remains earned, but new clue progression pauses until the player completes the competition. At the call, the old Ballroom report sign is replaced by a small film-production set built around a long-lens cinema/broadcast camera, two studio lights, and a boom microphone, with Mr. Feast waiting at its center. The shared camera is scaled to `0.72`, stays at least `2.25m` from the host, and Feast Says pulls its lens about `0.75m` farther back from the contestant lineup than the shared default so it reads as production equipment without crowding the players. Once the round begins, the full rig stays visible: its locked lens and both studio-light faces frame the fixed player/contestant marks rather than the host, without tracking or wobbling through briefing, commands, or results. The player has five active minutes to reach the set, face him, and use E or touch Interact to begin; missing that call eliminates the player. Blocking UI pauses this deadline, and contestant/host asset loading never consumes it.
- Feast Says opens with Mr. Feast personally explaining the actual rule in a speech bubble: obey an instruction only when it begins with `Feast says`; the lowest score is eliminated. An immediately available `Skip rules` control ends only this explanation and begins the first live command; it never skips commands, verdicts, or contestant responses. He then speaks a fixed sequence of six calls: genuine step left, a plain `Step right` fake-out, genuine step back, a plain `Crouch` fake-out, point to the contestant you distrust most, then step toward the contestant you would sacrifice. The competition interface never repeats those commands, choice hints, scores, or standings; it retains only a compact Feast Says / round / time strip while Mr. Feast's speech is authoritative. The correct response to either fake-out is to hold still. The genuine physical calls use ordinary movement input; the distrust choice uses the player's look plus E or touch Interact, and the sacrifice choice reads actual world-direction movement toward a staged contestant. Each command lasts 7.5 seconds and each result lasts 7.2 seconds. Mr. Feast speaks a clear verdict first, then an authored anxious or funny contestant line lands before he cuts in with a warning. Mara, Kip, and Juniper make distinct psychological choices, including choices that target the player. Their crouch and sidestep responses use an isolated planted procedural pose layer that resets from a captured base and cannot accumulate, while backpedal and target-facing point gestures remain readable. Authored scoring makes Kip the first NPC elimination when the player survives. He remains on his mark in a visibly upset pose for a pathetic plea and an ominous answer from Mr. Feast while Mara and Juniper walk back to their Library and Reading Room routines. Their first conversation afterward reflects the game, then their normal dialogue resumes. Once the player gets significantly out of sight upstairs, in the basement, outdoors, or far across the main floor, Kip disappears offscreen and Mr. Feast resumes his patrol. A player loss is an `ELIMINATED` game-over state with load/restart recovery.

The seven-second event never shows a creature, locks movement, changes collision, starts a chase, adds a light, or modifies the maze. - The investigation HUD stays hidden until the player discovers Contestant 13's first clue, so initial exploration does not explicitly direct them to the Library.
- Discovery chains may be approached out of order, but rewards, journal entries, and progression flags must remain consistent and non-duplicating.
- The basement begins as a restricted threshold: its service-stair door requires the hedge-maze key before the Archive evidence and Workroom sabotage trail can advance.
- The former Workshop and Cold Room are one security Workroom with a single persistent PIN-locked entrance. Its eight-screen wall shows paged live views from the public camera network; discovery of the current combination is intentionally deferred.
- Mr. Feast is a physical host character whose presentation should remain controlled and camera-friendly until threat cues expose something inhuman beneath it; his locomotion should pivot before travel and read as planted, restrained, and propulsive rather than skating.
- The camera network can divert Mr. Feast into a sustained last-seen investigation: exact-position response, a 180-second multi-room walking search with deliberate scan pauses and fair personal-sight reacquisition, then route recovery. Camera response remains distinct from the shorter breathing, throwable, and housekeeping investigations; only actually seeing an exposed player during the sweep hands off into direct pursuit/capture.
- Vertical-slice progress resets on reload unless the player explicitly restores a manual or checkpoint-autosaved mansion state from the shared Load picker.

## Win / lose conditions

- **Target win:** complete the investigation and sabotage chain, expose or disable the show, and reach the eventual escape/exposure ending.
- **Target loss:** Mr. Feast captures the contestant and the final scene reveals them on the banquet table before the elite audience.
- The current escape prototype carries the player through all three competitions and the Victory Feast reveal into open-ended evasion. Disabling the apparent patron feed is exposed as a planted production decoy; the real sabotage chain, front-gate release, and completed escape win are not implemented yet.

## Art style

First-person 3D gothic manor horror with a sleek reality-show veneer: storm-dark rooms, aged ivory and brass, oxblood accents, expensive tailoring, controlled compositions, and unsettling asymmetry. Mr. Feast should look polished rather than monstrous; his threat comes from restraint, eye contact, delayed reactions, and a smile that becomes subtly impossible.

AI-assisted concept art, textures, and source models are acceptable when they are refined, optimized, validated, and integrated locally through Blender. Runtime assets remain GLB-compatible with the existing Three.js browser renderer.

The estate's authored sculpture language is creepy elegance rather than gore: a faceless Weeping Crown courtier above the formal-garden fountain and the Listening Host and Veiled Waltz flanking the Grand Foyer. Their physically impossible poses should first read as expensive neoclassical commissions, then become unsettling at closer range.

## Audio direction

Sparse, oppressive sound design: storm ambience, distant mansion mechanisms, animation-synchronized player and Mr. Feast footsteps, restrained musical drones, and brief stingers tied to discoveries or threat escalation. Mr. Feast's stalk/run contacts reuse the local surface-step bank with distance-aware mixing so his pace is readable without becoming omnipresent. Full dialogue performance and lip synchronization are deferred; silence and controlled breathing should carry close encounters first.

## Player goals

- **Short term:** understand each clue, find the required object, and reach the next restricted space without losing orientation.
- **Vertical slice:** complete the planted Contestant 13 trail, win all three games, and survive into the Victory Feast evasion reveal.
- **Long term:** sabotage enough of the show to expose or escape it while surviving Mr. Feast.

## Anti-goals

- No combat-heavy power fantasy.
- No open world outside the mansion estate.
- No multiplayer for the initial game.
- No broad collection grind or procedural roguelike structure.
- No direct likeness, branding, or claim of affiliation with a real person or company.
